Migration and taxes

Who moves, and where to read the tax rules

Tax-return migration, Census survey estimates of the foreign-born population, and tax-agency pages are different records. They are shown separately here.

Foreign-born residents

The 2024 ACS five-year estimates count 973,325 foreign-born residents of North Carolina, 9.1% of that survey population, with a 90% margin of error of ±9,858 on the foreign-born count. This is not an annual immigration flow.

Census ACS 2024 5-year table B05002

State-to-state tax-return migration, filing years 2023–2024

The IRS Statistics of Income state-to-state files count income-tax returns that changed state between filing years 2023 and 2024. North Carolina received 169,694 returns in the published “Total Migration-US and Foreign” inflow total and sent 131,912 in the matching outflow total. The largest inbound state sources, after those file-wide totals and same-state rows, were Florida (21,177 returns), South Carolina (15,232), and Virginia (14,985). The largest outbound destinations were South Carolina (17,481), Florida (13,675), and Virginia (12,484). These are tax-return flows, not a Census change-of-residence estimate. AGI in the source file is in thousands of dollars.

Tax rules

Federal earned income tax credit rules are published by the Internal Revenue Service. North Carolina individual income tax forms, rate schedules, and bulletins are on the N.C. Department of Revenue individual income tax page. This page does not assert a North Carolina state earned-income credit, because that was not verified from the official pages reviewed for this release.
